# Digest: Mage: The Ascension 20th — Gods & Monsters

> Antagonist/bestiary + companion expansion. A scan-fast catalog of the opposition (and allies) so the GM can grab a ready NPC/creature mid-prep and drop it into `Cast/`. This is **content + a few subsystems**, not a rules replacement — see **Overrides**.

## System & source
- **Game & line:** Mage: The Ascension 20th Anniversary (M20)
- **Source:** *Gods & Monsters* (White Wolf / Onyx Path, 2019). "Creatures and Companions for Mage 20." Chapters 1–5 + Spirit Charms appendix (~pp. 12–220).
- **Type:** **Expansion** — NPC/creature roster, companion-creation rules, special advantages, and spirit Charms. Does **not** change the player's core resolution, condition track, or XP.
- **Dice subcommand:** `m20` (Attribute + Ability + any splat trait vs. difficulty; default diff 6). All stat blocks below use this same engine.

## How to read a stat block (and the competence tiers)
Every entry gives **Attributes / Abilities / Willpower / Health Levels / Armor Rating (soak dice) / Powers**, plus Image and Roleplaying Notes. Ephemeral spirits add **Rage / Gnosis / Essence (or Power)** and a **Charms** list instead of (or alongside) physical stats; the strongest also list **Equivalent Arete / Equivalent Spheres**.

**Reading the numbers fast — humans average Attribute 2, Ability 2–3, soak = Stamina.** Map onto `Cast/_template/sheet.md`:
- **Mook** — Attributes mostly 2–3, Abilities 2–3, WP 3–5, soak 0–2, normal 7-box Health. Loses to a competent PC. (Blood-Bound Ghoul, Vodou Zombi, Eye Scream, Creeper, Tiny Warrior.)
- **Capable** — Attributes 3–4 (a few 5s), Abilities 3–5, WP 5–7, soak 3–6, may have several extra Health boxes, 1–3 nasty powers. A real fight for one PC. (Witch-Hunter, Violent Young Vampire, Murderous Werewolf, Frankensteinian Monstrosity, most Personages.)
- **Elite** — Attributes 5–10+, Abilities 5–8, WP 8–10, soak 8–20, many extra Health boxes ("Terminated"/"Disabled" tracks), Sphere-equivalent or rote powers. Should be a set-piece, not a random encounter. (Ancient Master Vampire, HIT Mark IX "Iron Bob," Atlas unit, Talos, god-forms, Paradox Furies, Tzitzimimeh.)

**Special "extra-Health" tracks:** constructs/reanimates use longer tracks ending in *Terminated / Destroyed / Disabled / Crushed / Ick* etc. — read box count, not the labels, for toughness.

## CHAPTER 1 — Sleepers, Consorts, Hunters & Night-Folk (pp. 17–75)
Templates for the mortals and supernaturals who orbit mages. *Night-Folk are written "as mages see them" — deliberately incomplete; if you run real crossover, use the actual V20/W20 traits.*

**Sleepers & ordinary people** (pp. 18–49): age/role templates (Child, Teen, Senior, Hardcase, Homeless Survivor) and **Consorts/Acolytes** — the staff a mage keeps: Assistant, Backup (brute squad), Cleaner, Cultist, Handler, **Igor**, Lover, Professional, Servant, Slave. All **mook**-tier; use as henchmen, informants, hostages, retainers.

**Hunters & "Static" Adepts** (pp. 51–60) — *capable* anti-mage opposition:
- **Witch-Hunter** (Marcus Washington type) — sleepers who learned mages exist the hard way. No magic; win via ambush, rifles, IEDs, traps, teams. WP 8, good Firearms/Crafts (weaponsmith, traps). One alone is no match for a mage; a prepared team is lethal.
- **Bioenhanced Field Agent, Hedge Magician, Invested Infernalist, PDC Aberration Hunter, Spirit-Talker, New-Age Dilettante** — Technocracy/freelance flavors. Hedge magicians have minor "static magic"; infernalists borrow demonic Charms.

**The Night-Folk** (pp. 60–75) — other splats through mage eyes:
- **Vampires** — spend blood points/turn to boost Physical Attribute, heal, gain extra actions; bite = aggravated. Sunlight/fire = aggravated, can't be soaked easily; provoke frenzy (Willpower to control). *Tiers:* **Blood-Bound Ghoul** (mook, WP 3, 3 blood pts), **Violent Young Vampire** (capable, WP 5, 10 blood, claws/dominate), **Ancient Master** (**elite**: Attributes to 6, WP 9, 20–30 blood, 15–20 rote-dots costing 1 blood each, terror aura forcing a WP roll just to act against it). Named: Fallon Marx (ghoul), Lupita & Michael (young pack), "Uncle Robert" Castrovinci (~1,000-yr elder, Bronx warlord).
- **Werebeasts/Garou** — shift forms; war-form **doubles Physical Attributes**, App 0, ignores wound penalties (except silver), bite/claw Str+1/+2 aggravated. Rage pool (5–7) buys extra actions. **Silver = unsoakable aggravated.** Provoke Delirium in sleepers. *Capable→elite.* Named: Murderous Werewolf, Hunts-the-Wizards (anti-mage zealot), Enigmatic Werecat.
- **The Fae** (Jason "Goatboy"), **Ghosts** (Tragic Phantom, Raging Spectre, Ragdoll) — capable, mostly Mind/Spirit threats.

> **Night-Folk Mages?** (canon clarification, p. 61): mages cannot become vampires/werewolves and vice-versa; the Embrace shreds an Avatar. A mage *can* become a ghoul (losing magic over time). Other splats can't Awaken or use Spheres.

## CHAPTER 2 — Constructs & Familiars (pp. 77–101)
Things mages **build or conjure**. The combat-relevant heavies live here.

**Constructs**
- **Artificial People** (Darius, Masako 01) — passable humanoids, capable.
- **HIT Marks** (Technocracy cyborgs/robots) — **elite** muscle:
  - *Mark VI "Mercury"* / *Mark VII "Mask"* — shapeshifting infiltrators (Str 6, Stam 7, soak 10–11, Terminated track, 4-die countermagick). Vulnerable to Paradox/sand/cold ("freeze").
  - *Mark IX "Iron Bob"* — Str 10, Stam 10, soak 15, twin plasma cannons (dmg 8 aggravated), self-destructs (8 lethal in 4 yards). A Paradox magnet in Consensus reality. Walking boss fight.
  - **Atlas unit** (referenced, p. 81) — Str 12, Stam 10, soak 20, plasma cannon (10 dice aggravated), 5–10 Technocratic Procedures (Effects). End-of-arc threat.
- **MODES** (p. 84) — "manufactured celebrities," social/propaganda agents (App/Manip/Cha 5, Subterfuge/Media), Nephandic-infiltration flavor. Capable social antagonist, not a fighter.
- **Reanimates** (necromancy, pp. 85–88) — *all carry the Unbelief Flaw* (witnesses reject them):
  - **Eye Scream** (winged eyeball spy — mook), **Creeper** (gross-out shambler — mook), **Flayed Skin** (tormented conscious skin — mook), **Handy Hands** / **Talking Head** (utility), **Shambling Horror & Slaughterer** (capable killer, Brawl/Melee, Cause Insanity), **Frankensteinian Monstrosity** (capable→elite: Str 6, Stam 7, retains intellect, Rapid Healing, Ferocity, can soak aggravated — a vengeful recurring threat).
- **Soulflowers** (pp. 89–91) — living beings used as Quintessence batteries (Eva Cherone the unwitting human; **Lord Floof** the energy-draining cat). Mook bodies, plot fuel.
- **Talos** (pp. 91–92) — bronze giant, **elite**: Str 10, Stam 10, soak 16, Fire Blast (5 dice aggravated, burns over time), throws boulders. Mythic boss.
- **Thorn Gorgers** (p. 93) — carnivorous plant swarm, capable as terrain hazard: Stam 7, soak 8, vines (Str+1 lethal each, grapple at +2 diff to escape). Spread across the Umbra.
- **Tiny Warriors** (p. 94) — toy soldiers grown to full size on Arete-fueled activation; fearless mute fanatics. Mook squad (Str 5, soak 5+armor), run out of energy fast.
- **Zombies** (pp. 94–96): **Vodou Zombi** (mook, WP 0 vs. its creator, regenerates, killed by salt/fire/acid), **Cannibal Corpse** (capable, demon-driven, fast, Spirit 4 magick to purge).

**Familiars** (pp. 97–99) — materialized spirit companions (matter *and* ephemera). Built with physical stats **plus Charms**. **Daqqanoenyent / Uncle Whirlwind** — flying skull, 5-dot familiar, **elite**: WP 10, soak 8, Rage 5/Gnosis 5/Essence 30, huge Charm + Special Advantage list (Cause Insanity, Spirit Travel, Telekinesis). Also Lullabye, Tiberius. Use as powerful, prickly allies (or threats if mistreated).

## CHAPTER 3 — A Bestial Bestiary (pp. 103–121)
**Natural animals** (pp. 104–107): quick stat lines for packs/swarms and individuals — ape, bear (small/large), bull, boar, coyote, dolphin, elephant, octopus (up to boat-eater), shark, snake. Grab for familiars, ambushes, environmental danger. Mook→capable by size.

**Bygone Monsters** (pp. 108–121) — legendary cryptids, mostly *capable* set-dressing threats: **Anuk Ite, Aigamuxa, Black Snakes, Eloko, Grootslang, Inkanyamba, Jiangshi, Kijimuna, Kongamato, Ravening Winds, Shisa, Skymurmurs, Taniwha**, and the **Yao Gui** family (Hulijing, Inugami, Oni, Tanuki, Tengu). Folkloric monsters to seed regional flavor.

## CHAPTER 4 — Ephemeral Entities (pp. 123–179)
Spirits, Avatars, Paradox punishers, totems, and gods. Stat shape: **Willpower / Rage / Gnosis / Essence (or Power)**, **Charms**, materialized Attributes/Abilities/Health, sometimes **Equivalent Arete + Spheres**.

**Avatars** (pp. 124–131) — the mage's own inner spirit personified (Beloved, The Boss, Crush, Grandmother, Hunter, Mask, Stalks-The-Master, etc.). Story devices for Seekings, not combatants.

**The Djinn** (pp. 131–136) — fire/desert spirits, master illusionists and bargainers (immune to the Bargain Charm themselves): **al-Ghadib the Mad Warrior, Amira the Storyteller, Yazid al-Ghul** (feeds on grief). WP 6, Gnosis 8, Power 25–30, deep Charm lists (Break Reality, Mirage, Possession, Wish Fulfillment). Capable→elite tricksters.

**Lesser Entities (Umbrood)** (pp. 137–141) — the spirits a wandering mage actually meets:
- **Dataphyte** (mook — eats unattended thoughts/ideas; Essence 10), **Grinders** (Lord Viscount's tech-minion servitors), **Miengu** (benevolent water spirits, healing/luck), **Omniphage** (**elite** — car-sized devourer that eats matter/spells, Absorb Spell turns countermagick into Essence; Power 50), **Owl Shards** (fear/hate spirits that nest in abusive hosts → "Owl Walkers," Wyrm-flavored; Rage 8–10), **Qilin** (tranquil but lethal if provoked; Rage 8).

**Personages** (pp. 142–144) — named spirits with agendas, *capable→elite*: **Aelida, Lady of Feathers** (shapeshifting Dynamism spirit, anti-Technocracy, gives Tass; Equiv. Arete 6), **La Huesera the Bone Woman** (sings the forgotten dead back changed; Equiv. Arete 6, Spheres incl. Mind 5/Prime 4), **Lord Viscount Talos Perdix** (Umbrood Lord of technology, Rage 8/Gnosis 8/Essence 60, Hypertech patron — dangerous ally), **Tzitzimimeh** (Aztec star-goddesses, **elite**: WP 9, Essence 70, Equiv. Arete; protect women/devour men).

**Paradox Manifestations** (pp. 145–149) — Umbral punishers that hit mages who overreach (run these as *consequences*, not random foes):
- **Erinyes / The Furies** — Alecto the Mad, Megaera the Spiteful, Tisiphone the Relentless (Dex 8, Rage 5–9, Charms: Break Reality, Lightning Bolt, Terror, Temporal Loop). Elite punishment for entropic/murderous hubris.
- **Farandwee** (clown, Corr/Time — drags mages into a mirror-maze, +up to 10 difficulty), **Hex** (Entropy — forces botches), **Swipe** (Mind+Corr/Data — teleports an offending mage far away, +5 diff to act), **Whisper** (Mind/Life — never materializes; breeds paranoia from inside the mind).

**Totem Spirits** (pp. 150–154) — pack/group patrons: Cat, Cockroach, Joe Dread, Mama Plenty, Mr. Black, Trip, Wolf. Grant boons via the Totem Background.

**God-Forms & Greater Kami** (pp. 154–168) — true divinities (Anansi, Big Owl, Bondye, Christ, Itzpapalotl, Kane, Mawu-Lisa, Pan, Pele; Amaterasu, Hachiman, Ryujin, Susanoo, Tsukuyomi). **Elite/uncatchable** — reality-shaping plot powers, not stat-and-fight.

**The Loa** (pp. 169–179) — Vodou spirits (Legba, Baron Samedi, Oggun, Oshun, Oya, Shango, Yemaja, Aido-Hwedo). Patrons that possess, bless, and bargain via the Loa Background.

## CHAPTER 5 — Crafting Characters (pp. 181–213)
Rules for **building a non-mage companion/antagonist** when the templates don't fit.
- **Types:** Alien, Animal, Bygone, Construct, Familiar, Object, Reanimate, Robot, Spirit (p. 182).
- **Companion creation** + a guided question list (p. 189) for fleshing out an ally's life and ties to its mage.
- **Merits, Flaws, Special Advantages** (pp. 194–214) — the non-human toolkit. Key ones the GM will reach for:
  - **Flaws:** *Unbelief* (3/5/8 — witnesses reject the creature; mandatory for reanimates), *Power Source*, *Thaumivore*, *Broken*, *Limbless*, *Alien Impression*, *Omega/Beta/Alpha* (pack rank).
  - **Special Advantages (point-buy powers):** Armor, Claws/Fangs/Horns (3/5/7 = Str+1/+2/+3 lethal), Venom/Poison, Cause Insanity, Mesmerism, Shapechanger, Wings (flight), Telekinesis, Spirit Travel, Intangibility, Soak Lethal/Aggravated, Rapid Healing/Regrowth, Tides of Fortune, Mystic Shield, Paradox Nullification, Human Guise/Speech. **Use these to stat a custom creature fast: pick a tier, then bolt on 2–4 advantages.**

## Spirit Charms (pp. 214–219) — running ephemeral powers
- **What they are:** spirit powers fueled by **Essence**, not magick — they **do not invoke Paradox**. Minor spirits ≤5 Charms; greater spirits scale up; god-forms get Sphere-like effects. Full base list is in **Mage 20, pp. 489–495**; this book adds companion-oriented Charms.
- **Player-owned familiars** may buy Charms at **1 freebie per Essence point of activation cost** (a 3-Essence Charm = 3 freebies; "always-on"/luck/illusion Charms = 5 freebies). Optional rule — Storyteller may refuse.
- **Notable new Charms:** *Bad Luck Curse* / *Good Luck Charm* (Essence vs. diff 6, flips a target's next roll — black-cat staple), *Bargain* (djinn negotiation edge), *False Wealth* & *Mirage* (illusions of matter/sight-and-sound — pierced by Prime/Matter or high Perception), *Mislead* (cloud a decision), *Disorient*, *Jack In* (possess a phone/computer), *Plant Command*, *Rouse the Dead* (temporarily revive a corpse, ~5 Essence/body — godlike), *Sand Storm / Sand Swallow / Smoke Screen* (djinn/elemental area control), *Spirit Gossip* (gather Umbral info), *Teleport* (10 Essence, to a visited place), *Wish Fulfillment* (rarest djinn power, 30 Essence; **cannot raise a mage's Avatar/Arete/Spheres**).
- **Summoning/binding/warding** is handled in *How Do You DO That?* pp. 90–106 (referenced, not reprinted here).

## Overrides
- **This digest changes nothing about the player's core rules.** It is purely additive antagonist/ally **content** plus three optional **subsystems** (companion creation, Special Advantages, Spirit Charms).
- It does **NOT** override the M20 / CLAUDE.md defaults for: the d10 **core resolution** (Attribute+Ability vs. difficulty, 1s cancel, botch rule), the **Health/condition track** and soak, the **oracle**, or **XP/advancement**. Keep using M20 (or its core-book digest) for all of those.
- The only mechanics it introduces are **for NPCs/creatures**: extra-length Health tracks on constructs/reanimates (read the box count), spirit **Essence/Rage/Gnosis** pools + **Charms** (Essence-fueled, Paradox-free), and the **Special Advantage** point-buy menu. Use these when statting or running an entity from this book; they never touch the player's own sheet unless the Player builds a companion under the optional Chapter 5 rules.
- When a creature's power references a page in *Mage 20*, *How Do You DO That?*, or *The Book of Secrets*, those remain the authority for the underlying mechanic; this book just lists which power the creature has.
